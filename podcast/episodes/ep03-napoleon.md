# Episode 3: "Napoleon Was Tall. A British Cartoonist Ruined His Life."

**Runtime target:** 34 minutes
**Thumbnail text:** 5'7" ACTUALLY
**The B-Side angle:** Napoleon wasn't short. He was about average for a Frenchman of his time. His "tiny tyrant" image was created and spread by British satirical printmakers, above all James Gillray, during a propaganda war that the British government partly paid for. It may be the most successful smear campaign in history, because it's still working two hundred years later.
**The bet:** Nora bets Cal can't explain the difference between French and English inches in under 30 seconds. Loser wears a bicorne hat for the rest of the episode (on video).

## THE B-SIDE: ACT THREE, "THE SHRINKING MAN"

`[MUSIC: slower, reflective]`

**NORA:** So, the "Napoleon complex." Where does that come from?

**CAL:** The idea that short men overcompensate gets loosely linked to the psychologist Alfred Adler. In the early twentieth century Adler developed the concept of the "inferiority complex," the idea that feelings of inferiority, including about the body, can drive people to compensate. Adler didn't really put Napoleon's name on it. The phrase "Napoleon complex" is a later, popular label.

**NORA:** So it's pop psychology built on a cartoon.

**CAL:** And here's the funniest part. In 2007 a small study at the University of Central Lancashire put pairs of men in a stick-fighting game and measured how readily they retaliated when they were hit. If anything, the *taller* men were the ones more likely to lose their temper.

**NORA:** So the Napoleon complex might be backwards.

**CAL:** It's one small study, but it doesn't help the myth.

**NORA:** Okay, I want to get serious for a second, because I think this matters, and it's why we picked this episode. Napoleon did terrible things, and I'm not on Team Napoleon. He reinstated slavery in the French colonies in 1802. Millions of people died in his wars. If you want to criticize him, there's a *massive* list, and it's all real.

**CAL:** Absolutely.

**NORA:** And the thing everybody "knows" about him is the thing that *isn't* true. The one fake fact beat all the real ones. A cartoonist with a pencil in a shop window did more to shape how the world sees Napoleon than all his victories, all his laws and all his crimes put together.

**CAL:** It's the most effective piece of political propaganda in history, possibly. It's been running for over two hundred years, and it still works. Right now, someone listening is saying "but he *was* short."

**NORA:** He wasn't! Let the man be five foot seven!

**CAL:** James Gillray spent years of his life drawing one man. He drew Napoleon over and over, and turned him into the most famous cartoon character of the age. And he never once saw him.
