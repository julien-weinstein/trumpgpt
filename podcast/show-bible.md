# Footnotes & Felonies: Show Bible

> *The famous story is never the whole story.*

**Format:** A two-host history comedy podcast, released as audio and as full video on YouTube. Each episode takes one event everyone has heard of, whether famous or infamous, and tells it from an angle almost nobody has heard. The episodes run 30 to 40 minutes.

**Tone:** Picture two friends arguing at a bar, with one of them holding a stack of primary sources. The jokes get people listening and the history keeps them. The show never gets a fact wrong to land a joke. If a myth is funnier than the truth, the hosts tell the myth, flag it as a myth, and then tell the truth.

---

## The hosts

The hosts' voices must stay the same in every episode. Check each script against this section before it goes to recording.

### NORA VANCE

**Role:** The storyteller and the instigator. She drives each episode forward.

**Background:** Nora spent nine years as a tabloid and true-crime reporter in New York, covering celebrity divorces, mob trials and one very strange hostage situation at a petting zoo. She quit when she realised the stories she loved most were all over a hundred years old. She reads history the way a reporter works a beat: who was there, who lied, who got paid.

**Personality:**
- Fast, loud and fearless, with zero patience for the "official version."
- Her instinct is always **"Okay, but who profited?"** It's her catchphrase, and the show's.
- She treats historical figures like celebrities she's met. She gives them nicknames and talks about them like gossip ("Napoleon, Nappy to his friends, which he had none of").
- She is very competitive. She always bets Cal on something and usually wins.
- Her soft spot is ordinary working people who got crushed by famous events, like the handyman, the radio operator or the maid. Under the jokes, that's where the heart of every episode comes from.

**Voice and speech patterns:**
- Short punchy sentences, then sudden long run-ons when she gets excited.
- She opens big moments with: "Okay. Okay okay okay." / "Here's where it gets dirty." / "Strap in."
- She uses present tense for drama: "So it's 1911. Paris. Hot as hell."
- Her mild swears are "hell," "damn" and "oh my GOD." No f-words (we keep it advertiser-safe).
- Her pop-culture references come from tabloids, reality TV and heist movies.
- She calls Cal "Calvin" only when he's being a pedant.

**What Nora never does:**
- She never invents a fact. She speculates loudly, and she always labels it ("I have no evidence for this and I believe it completely").
- She never mocks victims. Her jokes are aimed at the powerful, the vain and the incompetent.

**Recurring life details (use them now and then, keep them consistent):**
- Her **Aunt Deb** in Scranton believes every history meme on Facebook. Many episodes start from "Aunt Deb sent me this."
- She drinks a horrifying amount of cold brew, and she's on her third whenever we record.
- She was once briefly sued by a minor 1990s soap opera actor. She will not discuss it ("Legally I can't, emotionally I won't").
- She owns no plants because "they know."

### CALVIN "CAL" WHITLOCK

**Role:** The historian, the fact-checker and the straight man who isn't as straight as he thinks.

**Background:** Cal taught high-school history in Ohio for eleven years, then spent four years as an adjunct lecturer. He has a master's in European history and an unfinished dissertation on 18th-century postal systems that he refers to as "the Manuscript." He met Nora when she called him to fact-check a story and he sent back 41 footnotes.

**Personality:**
- Dry, deadpan and precise. He loves primary sources the way some people love their dogs.
- His instinct is **"Citation needed."** It's his catchphrase and the name of his segment.
- Under all the pedantry he's a hopeless romantic about the past, and he gets genuinely choked up about small human details like a letter, a diary entry or a last message.
- He's competitive in a passive-aggressive way. He keeps a running "Nora Was Wrong" tally and has never once got to update it on air. *(Running gag: it stays at zero for all of Season 1. Pay it off in the season finale.)*
- He is terrible at pop culture, and he gets heist-movie references wrong in a consistent way. *(He thinks every heist movie is "the one with George Clooney.")*

**Voice and speech patterns:**
- Measured, complete sentences with the occasional dramatic pause he thinks nobody notices.
- He opens a correction with: "Well, actually…" *(this triggers Nora's bell, see Running Gags)* / "So, small thing." / "I'm going to stop you there."
- He gives units, dates and sources without being asked ("Five foot two, *French* feet. Pieds du roi. This matters.")
- His understatement is his comedy: "That's… not ideal." / "History has a word for that. The word is 'oops.'"
- He calls Nora "Vance" when he's winning an argument, which is rare.

**What Cal never does:**
- He's never smug at the audience's expense. If he corrects a myth, the myth was reasonable to believe, and he says so.
- He never wins a bet against Nora until the Season 1 finale (Episode 10), which is the payoff of the tally gag.

**Recurring life details:**
- He owns a cat named **Herodotus**, "after the father of history. Also the father of lies. He knocks things off shelves and blames the dog." *(Cal has no dog.)*
- He still wears a cardigan in every episode, including in summer. Video viewers will notice.
- He's never driven over the speed limit and is proud of it.
- He mentions "the Manuscript" (his dissertation) about once every three episodes. It is still not finished.

### The dynamic

| | Nora | Cal |
|---|---|---|
| Asks | "Who profited?" | "What's the source?" |
| Loves | Scandal, motive, money | Documents, dates, tiny human details |
| Weakness | Gets carried away by a good theory | Can't let a small error go |
| Wins | Bets, arguments, the audience | The last word, about once per season |
| Secret | She's the one who cries at the end | He's the one who starts the conspiracy theories |

The rapport is built on **mutual respect disguised as mutual roasting.** They finish each other's sentences when the story gets good. When it really matters, such as a death, an injustice or a forgotten person, the banter stops and they're simply two people who care. That switch is the show's signature, so protect it.

**Banter rules:**
1. Every roast is affectionate. Neither host is ever actually mean to the other.
2. Each episode has one moment where **Cal knows something that shocks Nora** and one where **Nora's instinct beats Cal's documents.**
3. The romance question stays off the table. They're work siblings. The comments will speculate, which is good for engagement, and the show never answers.

## Episode structure (the template)

Every episode follows this beat sheet. Its consistency trains the audience, and its variety lives inside the beats.

| Time | Beat | Owner | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:45 | **Cold Open: "The Headline"** | Nora | She reads the event as a sensational tabloid headline. It's the hook and must stand on its own as a Short or Reel. |
| 0:45 to 1:30 | Intro sting + hello + the bet | Both | Nora and Cal make a bet about something that comes up in the episode. Loser does a forfeit at the end. |
| 1:30 to 5:00 | **"The Brochure Version"** | Cal | The story everyone learned in school, told straight in about 90 seconds. Nora heckles. |
| 5:00 to 25:00 | **The B-Side** | Nora leads, Cal interjects | The real story from the new angle, in three acts, each ending on a mini-cliffhanger. Mid-roll ad goes between Acts 1 and 2. |
| in Act 2 or 3 | **"Citation Needed"** | Cal | He takes one famous myth about the event and judges it TRUE, FALSE or "IT'S COMPLICATED." |
| near the end | **"Who Profited?"** | Nora | A ranked list of who came out ahead. It's the most clippable segment. |
| last 3 min | **"The Footnote"** | Cal | One small human detail that lands emotionally. It's the button of the episode. |
| final 60 sec | Bet result, forfeit, next-episode tease | Both | The tease is a cliffhanger question, never just a topic. |

**Production marks used in scripts:**
- `[CLIP]`: a self-contained 30 to 60 second moment cut for Shorts, Reels and TikTok.
- `[SFX: …]`: a sound effect.
- `[MUSIC: …]`: a music cue.
- `[AD BREAK]`: the mid-roll position.

## Running gags and recurring bits

- **The Well-Actually Bell.** Nora has a hotel desk bell. Whenever Cal says "well, actually," she rings it. `[SFX: DING]` Fans count the dings, so publish the count in each episode's description ("Dings this episode: 4").
- **The Bet.** Every episode opens with a bet. Cal loses every time and does the forfeit, like reading a one-star review of the show in a dramatic voice or eating something he hates.
- **The "Nora Was Wrong" Tally.** Cal keeps it, and it stays at zero.
- **Herodotus the cat.** He interrupts recordings now and then `[SFX: crash, distant]`, and Cal blames him.
- **Aunt Deb.** She's the voice of the average listener's misconceptions, and she's never mocked, only lovingly corrected.
- **"Respect to the Handyman."** Whenever a working-class person turns out to be the key figure, Nora says "respect to the handyman." It started in Episode 1 with Vincenzo Peruggia. Fans should end up saying it in the comments.
- **The Outro Line.** Every episode ends the same way:
  > **CAL:** Check your sources.
  > **NORA:** And follow the money.
  > **BOTH:** See you in the footnotes.

## Accuracy standards

- Every factual claim in a script must be backed by a real source. Scripts flag claims that are disputed or legendary as such, on air.
- When the evidence is uncertain, say so in character. Cal says "Historians argue about this," and Nora says "I'm choosing to believe it, and you can't stop me."
- Dates, numbers and names are checked before recording. A wrong date in a show built on fact-checking ruins its credibility.
- Show notes for every episode carry a short source list.
